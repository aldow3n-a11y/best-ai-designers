01 — Generative Image

The image model that reads a creative brief.

Most AI image tools default to "stock-photo-with-awe" — cinematic lighting, smiling people, golden hour. That's not what a working designer needs. Adobe Firefly is the rare exception: it's the only model trained exclusively on licensed Adobe Stock + public-domain content, so the IP is clean for client work, and it integrates directly into Photoshop, Illustrator, and Express. Generate the hero, then refine it in the tool you already use.

Who it's for

  • Agency designers who need commercial-safe imagery on tight deadlines
  • In-house brand teams with strict IP-compliance requirements
  • Illustrators using Generative Fill to extend or re-compose their own work

What it costs

Free tier (25 generative credits/mo). Firefly Standard $4.99/mo (100 credits). Firefly Pro $9.99/mo (500 credits). Bundled free with Creative Cloud paid plans.

What it doesn't do well

  • Photorealistic humans with consistent faces (use Midjourney for that)
  • Highly stylized illustration (Krea is better for painterly work)
  • Text in images (still unreliable — design type in Illustrator after)

02 — Vector + Illustration

The AI illustrator that exports real SVG.

Every "AI illustrator" until 2024 produced raster JPEGs and called it a day. Krea flips the script: it generates real vector paths, real strokes, real editable shapes. Feed it a sketch and it returns a finished illustration that you can ungroup, recolor, and re-export in Illustrator without a trace of bitmap. The inpainting is also uncanny — paint over a face and Krea respects the existing color palette.

Who it's for

  • Illustrators building editorial spots and brand mascots
  • Designers producing vector icon sets at scale
  • Anyone who got burned by an "AI illustrator" that exported 4000px PNGs

What it costs

Free tier (limited generations, low priority). Pro $10/mo (fast queue, higher res, commercial license). Studio $30/mo (API + team features).

What it doesn't do well

  • Photorealistic portraits (it's stylistically biased toward illustration)
  • Multi-character scenes with consistent character design (one-off only)

05 — AI Web Design

The site builder that ships a real page in 60 seconds.

Framer AI is the only AI website builder in 2026 that doesn't produce a Wix-with-stock-photos vibe. Describe your business, pick a layout direction, and Framer generates a real, responsive, on-brand landing page with actual components, real CMS hooks, and production-ready TypeScript. You can edit any element in the canvas afterwards, or just publish and ship.

The 60-second generation is genuinely fast — but the real value is the CMS + localization + A/B testing infrastructure you get for free. A freelance designer can hand a client a fully editable site by end of day one, instead of week three.

Who it's for

  • Designers who want to deliver client sites faster without writing code

What it costs

Free tier (1 site, framer.ai subdomain). Mini $5/mo (1 site, custom domain). Basic $15/mo (CMS + forms). Business $35/mo (multi-site, team, analytics). Compare to Webflow ($23/mo CMS) or Squarespace ($18/mo).

10 — FAQ

Questions designers actually ask.

Are these tools safe for client work?
All eight tools have commercial licenses for work you create in them (paid tiers). For Midjourney, Firefly, and Adobe generative tools, the IP stays with you. For Framer AI sites and Uizard mockups, you own the exported design. Always check the per-tool commercial terms before delivering final assets to a paying client.
Will AI replace designers?
AI replaces the boring 60% of a designer's day — mood boards, stock-photo searches, asset resizing, Figma cleanup, mockup variants. The remaining 40% — concept, taste, brand judgment, client iteration — is still human. Designers who use these tools ship 3x more concepts per week and charge higher rates because iteration is faster.
Which one is best for a solo creative on a budget?
Start with Framer ($15/mo) for landing pages + Canva free tier for social graphics + Krea free tier for mood-board explorations. That's a $15/mo stack that covers 80% of freelance designer output. Add Midjourney ($10/mo) when clients start asking for hero imagery you can't shoot yourself.
What about Figma Make or Figma AI?
Figma Make (the AI prompt-to-design feature) is genuinely useful inside an existing Figma file for auto-layout cleanup and component suggestions. But for from-scratch AI generation — hero images, illustrations, brand mood boards — you still need a dedicated tool like Midjourney or Firefly. The 8 on this list complement Figma; they don't replace it.
